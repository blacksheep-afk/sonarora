How did MOYA calculate this?

MOYA compares possible outcomes using the amounts and timing entered. Expected money is not treated as cash until it clears. If a payment date is unknown, the payment is left out until clarified. These examples are hypothetical.

Transparent calculation rules

Buy-now stock is committed on Day 0 from confirmed opening funds. The fixed wait strategy considers stock the day after the essential due date. The new receipt-triggered rule buys only after the expected payment actually clears, only when no shortfall has occurred, and only when cash can still cover remaining essential costs plus the stock. In every case, cleared receipts arrive before obligations due that day; no purchase is silently retried.

1 · Received vs expected

Only cleared funds count as available. An expected payment is a separate outcome with an explicit arrival date and probability. If its date is unknown, it is excluded and flagged.

2 · Productive spending

Stock is shown as an outflow that may enable sales later. Sales are conditional on stock being bought and are counted only when received.

3 · Shortfall

For each obligation: shortfall = amount due − funds available before payment, floored at zero. Any unpaid amount remains visible; it is never silently paid.

4 · Receipt-triggered guardrail

This strategy reacts to a cleared receipt instead of treating an expected payment as cash. It does not buy while any earlier essential or emergency amount remains unpaid, and it keeps enough cash for later modeled costs before unlocking stock.

5 · Uncertainty relationship

Independent outcomes use multiplied probabilities. Linked-market mode puts more weight on both outcomes succeeding or both failing, within mathematically valid bounds.
